Allen Wilson

PhD Candidate (ABD), Department of Political Science
University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

I study how political attitudes are formed, organized, communicated, and translated into public policy. Situated at the intersection of American politics, political psychology, and public opinion, my research addresses three connected questions: how socialization and latent predispositions shape political judgment; how citizens structure their beliefs and evaluate political arguments; and how public preferences interact with institutions and policy outcomes.

These questions motivate projects on democratic attitudes, political persuasion, belief-system structure, elite rhetoric, capital punishment, public evaluations of the federal bureaucracy, and political mobilization. My dissertation develops familiar aphorisms as measures of durable predispositions and examines their use in political communication.

My work combines survey experiments with computational text analysis, psychometric network analysis, causal mediation, and multilevel regression and poststratification. Collaborative research includes an article in Public Opinion Quarterly, multiple manuscripts under review, and a Cambridge Element under contract.
