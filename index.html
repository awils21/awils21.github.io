About

Political judgment begins before politics.

I am an American politics scholar with interests in political psychology, public opinion, and methodology. Much of my work starts from a simple premise: people bring habits, values, and socialized dispositions from ordinary life into the political world.

My research connects those predispositions to democratic behavior, persuasion, belief-system structure, and policy outcomes. I use survey experiments, computational text analysis, psychometric networks, and other quantitative tools to study questions that are difficult to reach with conventional measures.

Research

Research agenda

Three connected questions

Full research profile →
01

Socialization and predispositions

Where political judgments originate

My dissertation uses common aphorisms to study durable orientations formed outside politics and examines how those familiar sayings function in elite communication. Related work investigates competitiveness and democratic responses to electoral defeat.

02

Beliefs and persuasion

How opinions are organized and changed

I examine partisan expectations in citizen-to-citizen persuasion and use network analysis to trace the changing organization of mass political belief systems.

03

Opinion and policy

When public preferences govern

I study how public attitudes translate into state action, including work on racial resentment and capital punishment and a developing project on public evaluations of the federal bureaucracy.
