Teaching

Teaching

My teaching is organized around intellectual curiosity, analytical rigor, and the active practice of political science. I want students to learn how to turn assumptions into questions, connect theoretical arguments to evidence, and distinguish conclusions that are well supported from those that remain provisional.

I pair demanding course objectives with transparent expectations, structured opportunities for practice, and detailed feedback. This approach maintains high academic standards while recognizing that students enter political-science courses with different substantive knowledge, methodological preparation, and educational experiences.

Teaching philosophy

Political science is especially well suited to a classroom built around inquiry. I encourage students to question familiar accounts of political events, identify the assumptions underlying competing explanations, and revise their views when the evidence warrants it. I also model intellectual humility by treating uncertainty as a reason for further investigation rather than as a failure of expertise.

Students develop these habits most effectively when they participate in the work of the discipline. My courses therefore use structured discussion, document-based analysis, writing assignments, simulations, survey design, and quantitative analysis. These activities make institutional processes and methodological choices concrete while requiring students to explain how they reached a conclusion.

I give students meaningful discretion over topics and applications so they can connect course material to questions they find important. At the same time, assignments are scaffolded around explicit learning objectives and common standards of argument and evidence. Detailed feedback helps students identify both what is working and what they should improve in subsequent work.

A productive classroom also requires access to guidance. I invite questions, provide multiple forms of participation, and make time for individual support when students encounter academic or personal obstacles. Flexibility and rigor are not competing goals: thoughtful support enables more students to meet ambitious expectations.

Courses taught

POLI 100

Introduction to American Government and Politics

Instructor of record · Fall 2025 and Fall 2026

This course introduces the constitutional foundations, institutions, and political processes of the United States while asking students to connect formal rules to contemporary outcomes. Guided discussion, close engagement with foundational and modern texts, analytical reflections, and simulations of institutional procedures help students evaluate competing explanations of American politics rather than simply memorize institutional facts.

POLI 209

Analyzing Public Opinion

Instructor of record · Summer 2025

Students study substantive theories of public opinion and political psychology alongside survey design, statistical reasoning, and data analysis. The course culminates in a collaborative research project in which students formulate hypotheses, build and field a survey in Qualtrics, analyze the resulting data in R, and assess what can be inferred given the design and sample.

Undergraduate research assistant team

Wilson Research Lab

Since spring 2025, I have directed the Wilson Research Lab, a team of twelve undergraduate research assistants. Students have contributed to the development of an original aphorism catalog and to the larger research program built around that resource.

I treat the lab as sustained training in collaborative social science. Students learn how individual research tasks fit a broader theoretical argument, why documentation and quality control matter, and how careful division of labor can produce evidence that no researcher could assemble alone.
